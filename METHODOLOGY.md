# Research Methodology

## Cyber Harassment & Stalking in Maldives: Legislative Gap Analysis

**Course:** UFCFJJ-15-M Social Media and Web Science  
**Date:** December 2025

---

## Research Design

This study employs a **mixed-methods approach** combining:
- Quantitative analysis of documented harassment cases
- Qualitative content analysis of harassment types and impacts
- Comparative legislative analysis

---

## Data Collection Methods

### 1. Primary Data Collection

**Source Identification:**
- International media outlets (Al Jazeera, Human Rights Watch, JURIST)
- Human rights organizations (IFJ, CPJ, FORUM-ASIA)
- Maldivian news sources (Maldives Independent, Atoll Times, Sun.mv)
- Parliamentary inquiry reports (Westminster Foundation for Democracy)

**Sampling Strategy:**
- **Purposive sampling** of documented cases from 2019-2025
- Inclusion criteria:
  - Publicly reported cases
  - Verified by credible sources
  - Occurred in Maldives or targeting Maldivians
  - Related to cyber harassment, stalking, or online violence

**Data Points Collected:**
- Date of incident
- Platform/method used
- Type of harassment
- Target demographic
- Source URL
- Contextual notes

**Total Cases:** 30 documented incidents

---

### 2. Secondary Data Collection

**Official Statistics:**
- Maldives Police Service cybercrime reports
- Bank of Maldives financial crime data
- Westminster Foundation parliamentary study (n=54)
- Media freedom indices

**Legislative Documents:**
- Penal Code amendments (December 17, 2025)
- Criminal Procedure Code amendments
- Media Bill (Act No. 16/2025)
- Budapest Convention documentation

## Limitations

### Data Limitations
1. **Underreporting:** Many cases likely unreported due to fear or lack of legal recourse
2. **Language Barrier:** Some Dhivehi-language sources may be missed
3. **Recent Events:** 2025 data represents partial year
4. **Selection Bias:** Media-reported cases may not represent all harassment types

### Methodological Limitations
1. **Cross-sectional Design:** Cannot establish direct causality
2. **Retrospective Data:** Relies on accuracy of past reporting
3. **Small Sample Size:** 30 cases limits statistical power
4. **Regional Comparison:** Different legal systems complicate comparison
